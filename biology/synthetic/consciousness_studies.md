## 1. Theoretical Foundations

### Integrated Information Theory (IIT)

#### Core Principles
Integrated Information Theory, developed by Giulio Tononi, proposes that consciousness corresponds to the capacity of a system to integrate information. The theory provides a mathematical framework for quantifying consciousness.

#### Key Concepts
- **Φ (Phi)**: The measure of integrated information
- **Information Integration**: The extent to which a system is more than the sum of its parts
- **Causal Power**: The ability of a system to cause effects on itself
- **Exclusion Principle**: Only the maximally irreducible conceptual structure (MICS) constitutes conscious experience

#### Mathematical Framework
```
Φ = max[cause-effect repertoires] × log₂(Effective Information)
```

Where effective information measures the selectivity of the cause-effect repertoire.

### Global Workspace Theory (GWT)

#### Core Principles
Global Workspace Theory, proposed by Bernard Baars, suggests that consciousness arises from information being globally available across multiple brain regions.

#### Key Components
- **Global Workspace**: A metaphorical "stage" where information becomes globally available
- **Spotlight of Attention**: Limited capacity for conscious processing
- **Broadcasting**: Global availability of conscious information
- **Ignition**: Sudden widespread activation when information becomes conscious

### Predictive Processing

#### Core Principles
The brain (or conscious system) constantly generates predictions about sensory input and updates its internal models based on prediction errors.

#### Mathematical Framework
```
Free Energy = Surprise + Complexity
```

The system seeks to minimize free energy through prediction and model updating.
